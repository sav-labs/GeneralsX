### FSR 3 upscaler and frame generation (opt-in)
- New `Upscaler = FSR3` (Options.ini) / `-upscaler fsr3`, and `FrameGeneration = yes|no` (default no, FSR 3 only) / `-framegen on|off`. Defaults and the FSR 1.0, OFF and FSR 2 paths are unchanged. Render only: the game logic, its frame rate, determinism and replays are not touched.
- SDK: AMD FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4 (FSR 3.1.4, MIT), the newest 3.1 release with GLSL shaders and a Vulkan backend. The Windows x64 workflow sparse-clones the pinned tag (`sdk/include`, `sdk/src`), copies it to `src/dxvk/fsr3/ffx-sdk` and ships `FidelityFX-SDK-LICENSE.txt` next to `d3d9.dll`. Without the SDK, DXVK builds as before and `DXVK_UPSCALER=fsr3` logs that FSR 3 is unavailable and uses FSR 2.
- DXVK patch `0016-fsr3-upscaler.patch`: the FSR 3.1 upscaler (fsr3upscaler component plus the SDK's Vulkan backend) takes the same inputs as FSR 2: camera motion vectors from depth, jitter, reactive mask, and the FSR 2 prepare and composite shaders, so the interface still comes from the EASU image. The 22 upscaler permutations (Lanczos, sharpening, low-res motion vectors), 11 frame interpolation and 7 optical flow passes are compiled with glslang into SPIR-V tables with our own `ffxGetPermutationBlobByIndex` and SPIR-V reflection, like FSR 2. A force-included shim hides device extensions the backend would use (fp16 permutations are not compiled, no wave64), renames the backend symbols that clash with the FSR 2 library, and defers the backend's image view destruction until the command list that used the views has completed. - DXVK patch `0017-fsr3-frame-generation.patch`: FidelityFX optical flow and frame interpolation are used directly, not the SDK swap chain replacement. The interpolation runs between the previous and current upscaled 3D scene (the upscaler's dilated depth and motion vectors are shared with it); the composite pass then draws the interface of the real frame over the generated one, so the interface is never interpolated. The D3D9 swap chain presents each frame twice: a Generated pass (upscale, interpolate, present the generated frame) and a Real pass (present the real frame upscaled by the first pass). The real frame is presented half a frame interval (moving average, at most 20 ms) after the generated one; `DXVK_FSR3_FRAMEGEN_PACING=0` turns that off. With a frame latency tracker or fewer than 3 swap chain images, frame generation is turned off with an `[FSR3] frame generation disabled: ...` line. `DXVK_FSR3_DEBUG=tearlines|view|resets` shows the FidelityFX debug overlays.

### Effect lights: explosions, fires, muzzle flashes and exhausts light the ground and units
- Why so few effects lit anything: the only automatic light source is the `LightPulse` FXList nugget, and only the FXLists whose data has one get a light. Particle systems have no light at all. Terrain receives `W3DDynamicLight`s per vertex on the 10 unit height grid (linear falloff, Gouraud diamonds, clamped at 1); meshes get them through `LightEnvironmentClass` (max 4 lights, usually 3 are the map's global lights, so the strongest point light gets the free slot).
- `EffectLights` (Core GameClient) classifies effects from what the data already says: camera shake strength, scorch radius, particle system names, and FXList naming (`...Detonation`, `...Death`, `...MuzzleFlash`, `...GunFire`). Kinds: muzzle, impact, explosion, large explosion, superweapon, flame; tints for toxin, EMP, laser, particle cannon and nukes. FXLists with a `LightPulse` keep their retail light and get nothing extra. The profile is worked out once per FXList (name kept at parse) and `FXList::doFXPos/doFXObj` hand it to `Display::addEffectLight` after the existing shroud check. Running particle systems that are not one-shot and look like fire or exhaust (name plus additive or fire-coloured particles, colour from their brightest colour key) become continuous lights with flicker, following the emitter (`ParticleSystem::getWorldPosition`) and fading with the particle count; shrouded or hidden ones are skipped.
- `W3DEffectLights` (Core device) owns 16 `W3DDynamicLight`s flagged as managed (`getADynamicLight` no longer hands them to retail pulses). Each drawn frame: pulse envelopes (attack, hold, squared decay on a clock that stops while paused), camera frustum cull, sort by priority, merge lights closer than half the larger radius, budget 16 (High) or 6 (Low). Objects: diffuse = colour x intensity, ambient 0.45 of it, range (0.25 R, R); scaled 0.55 in bright daylight up to 1 at night from the map's terrain light.
- Ground at High: the lights skip the terrain vertex path and `BaseHeightMapRenderObjClass::drawScorches` calls `drawTerrainSplats`: the terrain grid cells under each light (same diagonals as W3DScorch, 1 unit float, at most 64 vertices across) with a 64x64 falloff texture `(1-x^4)^2/(1+3x^2)` and blend `DESTCOLOR, ONE`: dest x (1 + L/V) per channel with V the map's sun plus ambient on flat ground, i.e. albedo x (V + L). Ratios above 1 use 2 or 3 passes of the n-th root (up to 8x on dark maps). Vertex colour also carries a slope term toward a lifted light point. Drawn before the shroud pass, so shroud still covers it.

### Water: reflections, shoreline foam, soft shore, waves
- Problem: Zero Hour draws standing water (water polygon triggers, `drawTrapezoidWater`) and rivers (`drawRiverWater`) as flat translucent polygons with one tiled texture: no reflections, and a hard line where the plane cuts the terrain. The retail mirror (`renderMirror`) only exists for water type 2, which Zero Hour maps do not use. On the Windows x64 build the river/trapezoid pixel shaders are not even created (`D3DXAssembleShader` is a stub), so the water runs fixed function.
- New `W3DWaterEffects.cpp` (Core, both games), all fixed function so it works on every D3D8 device and through DXVK without a new patch; extra passes over the same water polygons, render only:
  - Planar reflection (`WaterReflections = Off | Sky | Full`, default Full; `WaterReflectionScale` 0.25..1, default 0.5): before the main scene the water polygons are clipped to the camera frustum, the visible ones give a screen rectangle and the height of the largest one is the mirror plane. The camera is mirrored across it (as `renderMirror`) and the scene is drawn into an X8R8G8B8 render target of scale x the depth buffer size, but only the water's screen rectangle (viewport and view plane cut to it, plus a margin for the wave distortion). New `CameraClass::Set_Oblique_Clip_Plane` tilts the near plane of the projection onto the water plane (Lengyel), so the lake bed and anything under the water never shows in the mirror, also through the tree vertex shader (no user clip planes needed). `WW3D::Begin_Offscreen_Render/End_Offscreen_Render` bracket the pass without stepping the frame counter (particle buffers double buffer on it), statistics or dynamic buffer resets. A static camera refreshes the mirror every second frame. The mirror draws every drawable (retail mirrors only `CAN_CAST_REFLECTIONS` / mirror flagged ones), never hidden, shrouded or fading (stealth) ones, and skips shadows, ambient occlusion, the FSR 2 opaque copy and particles; mirrored drawables get their occlusion flags cleared (stale flags left objects out). Sky mode draws only a cloud layer of the time of day 150 units above the water.
  - Water pass: stage chain [wave du/dv BUMPENVMAP] -> reflection texture projected with the camera (CAMERASPACEPOSITION, COUNT3 | PROJECTED, projection x bias) -> Fresnel LUT (CAMERASPACEREFLECTIONVECTOR dotted with the water normal gives cos theta, F = 0.12 + 0.6 (1 - c)^2) -> shore alpha -> shroud. Blend factor = Fresnel x shore x TFACTOR, which fades the reflection out for water more than 24 units off the mirror plane (other lakes, sloped rivers).
  - Shore texture (`ShoreFoam = yes|no`, default yes): when the map loads (and at most once a second when scripts move water), water depth = water height (scanline fill of the water polygons, highest wins, a river's highest point) minus terrain height (same triangle interpolation as `getHeightMapHeight`), sampled every 5 world units over the map (up to 2048^2). Alpha = smoothstep(0, 5, depth): the base water pass multiplies its alpha with it on stage 1 (free on the fixed function path), so the shore fades in instead of a hard cut. RGB = foam band (rises over 0.8 units of depth, fades out from 1.5 to 7).
  - Foam pass: two layers of a 256^2 procedural foam texture (domain warped cellular lace over value noise patches with small bubbles, generated at start up) drift slowly in different directions (110 and 150 unit tiles), MODULATE2X, times the band and the time of day light, screen blended (ONE, INVSRCCOLOR) so it never clips.
  - Waves (`WaterWaves = yes|no`, default yes): 32 frame looping V8U8 du/dv animation (64^2, two layers of sines with integer wave numbers travelling east and north-west, 10 fps, 64 unit tile, slow drift) on a BUMPENVMAP stage in front of the reflection (0.012 screen offset) and the first foam layer.
  - Feathered water (`m_featherWater`) gets the effects on its top layer only; the legacy pixel shader path keeps its look but gets the reflection and foam passes.
- `WaterReflections = Off`, `ShoreFoam = no`, `WaterWaves = no` give the retail picture. `[WATER]` stderr lines report the settings, the reflection texture and shore texture builds. PerfStats: `water_reflection_ms` section, `water_refl=` on the `[PERF]` line and in the CSV. INSTALL.txt documents the keys.
- Cost: one extra scene pass at quarter pixel count (0.5 scale) limited to the water's screen rectangle, only while water is in view, every other frame for a still camera; two extra passes of a few hundred triangles; textures: 32 x 8 KB waves, 340 KB foam, up to 16 MB shore (1 MB for a 256 x 256 cell map).
- Checked: changed files compile with mingw (Zero Hour; Generals scene and camera with the base game include paths). Headless preview of a synthetic shore with the same textures, constants and pass order (`/mnt/project-files/plans/previews/water-*.png`). Not yet run on a GPU.
- Limits: one mirror plane per frame (other water heights fade out), the grid mesh water (`renderWaterMesh`) and the type 2 sea are unchanged, the legacy pixel shader water gets no soft shore, options are read when the game starts, devices with fewer than 5 texture stages keep retail water.

### Close-up terrain: detail texture, sun-lit relief, soft layer blends
- New `W3DTerrainDetail` (Core, both games) with a ps.1.4 terrain shader (`TerrainShaderDetail`, first in `TerrainShaderList`) that replaces the retail terrain pixel shader in the same single pass: no extra draw calls, five texture stages (atlas twice, cloud, light map, detail). Render only; logic, CRCs and pathing are untouched.
- Detail: a procedural tileable 1024x1024 value noise texture (built at load), one repeat per 64 world units, rotated by atan(0.5) against the cell grid so it does not line up with tiles; its mips fade to neutral grey, so it is gone at the default far view. Options.ini `TerrainDetail = yes|no` (default yes), `TerrainDetailStrength` 0..1 (default 0.7).
- Relief (`TerrainRelief`, default yes): when the atlas is 32 bit, `TerrainTextureClass::update` derives a height field from each texture class (half resolution luminance, blurred, wrapping), lights its normal with the map's GlobalLighting terrain lights, normalizes the mean to 1 and stores it in the unused atlas alpha; the shader multiplies the tile colour with it (fades out in the smaller mips). Rebaked from `staticLightingChanged` when the lights change.
- Soft blends (`TerrainSoftBlend`, default yes): the per-vertex layer blend alpha is shaped by a noise stored in the detail texture alpha, so straight cell-edge transitions become irregular. The three-texture blend tiles (`renderExtraBlendTiles`) use a second shader variant with the same detail and edge shaping.
- All three keys `no` keeps the retail shader list (exact retail look). No ps.1.4 or shader creation failure also falls back to it.
- Shaders are hand assembled token arrays (`D3DXAssembleShader` is a stub); new `scripts/tooling/shaders/assemble_ps1x.py` assembles ps.1.x. Both shaders were checked with DXVK's DXSO decoder/compiler and `spirv-val`. Changed files compile with mingw (Zero Hour). Previews from a numpy model of the shader in `plans/previews/terrain-*.png`. Not yet run on a GPU.
- Limits: relief needs the 32 bit atlas (no texture reduction); the low LOD `FlatHeightMap` path is unchanged; relief light direction is approximate on stretched cliff tiles; a few hundred ms more at map load.

### Real shadows for skinned units (SkinnedShadows)
- Problem: the shadow volume code skips skin meshes (`W3DShadowGeometry::initFromHLOD`), so skinned units of 5-15k triangles got no shadow or the flat round decal. New `W3DSkinnedShadowManager` (Core, both games) gives them a shadow of their current pose. Options.ini `SkinnedShadows = Projected | Volume | Decal` (default Projected; Decal = retail), `SkinnedShadowQuality = Low | Medium | High` (default Medium), `SkinnedShadowDistance` (1500, fade over the last quarter, 0 = off), `SkinnedShadowKeepDecal` (no), `SkinnedShadowMinTriangles` (2000).
- Which models: a model with skinned caster meshes whose data asks for a shadow volume, or for a shadow decal with at least `SkinnedShadowMinTriangles` triangles (retail infantry keep their decals), and shadow volume models over `ShadowVolumeMaxTriangles` (instead of #62's decal). The retail decal is dropped unless `SkinnedShadowKeepDecal = yes`. Volume typed shadows follow the 3D Shadows option, decal typed ones the 2D option.
- Projected (default): one render target atlas (R5G6B5, 2048 with 128 texel tiles at Medium; 64/1024 Low, 256/4096 High). Before the main scene each visible shadow is checked: a pose key (12 floats per pivot: rotation and position relative to the root, plus the hidden flags of the caster meshes) ignores pure driving; the tile is redrawn only when the pose changed, at most once per 2 frames (3 Low, 1 High) and 16 tiles per frame (8/32), oldest first. A tile is the posed caster seen along the sun direction (same light as the shadow volumes, `LightPosWorld[0]`), drawn as pretransformed triangles in the shadow colour on white; skin positions come from #62's skin cache, so an unchanged pose is not skinned again. In `DoShadows` the terrain cells under each shadow are clipped on the CPU to the column of light the tile covers and drawn in one multiplicative pass (texture + fade gray); bridges like the retail decals.
- Volume: per model edge table built once from the stored vertices, welding only within one bone (bone space positions); per shadow the silhouette of the posed mesh is extruded along the sun direction relative to the root, so a moving unit only changes its world transform. Over 4000 caster triangles (1500/12000) a vertex clustered proxy is used. Rebuilds at most 8 per frame (4/16) inside the retail stencil pass, which now also runs when only skinned volumes are visible.
- Route choice: Projected is the default because its CPU cost per redraw is a vertex transform of the skinned positions, redraws are budgeted and the ground pass is one draw call; Volume costs about 75 us per 15k triangle rebuild (21 us with the proxy) plus stencil fill, but shades other objects and the unit itself.
- Limits: projected shadows fall only on the ground and bridges (not on other units, buildings or the unit itself); the tile lags one frame behind the animation; overlapping projected shadows multiply (darker), stencil shadows do not; very low sun clips the ground area to 64x64 cells.
- Diagnostics: `[SHADOW]` stderr lines at start (settings), atlas creation, the first shadow of each model (caster meshes, triangles, route), the first volume of each mesh (edges, proxy) and every 600 frames (drawn, tile updates, volume updates, deferred, terrain triangles, ms, tiles in use). PerfStats: `skinned_shadows_ms` section and `skinshadows` / `updates` counters on the `[PERF]` line, the overlay and the CSV.
- Checked: `scripts/qa/perf/run-skinned-shadow-test.sh` (header only geometry, synthetic 13936 triangle 3 bone tank): the bind pose topology equals a rebuild from the posed mesh, silhouette loops close, the z-pass stencil count matches ray casts on 2925 ground samples (0 mismatches), the volume is unchanged when the unit only drives, the proxy agrees on 99.5% of the samples, clipped terrain covers exactly the light square and maps to the right tile coordinates. The changed engine files compile with mingw (Zero Hour; Generals with the base game include paths). Not yet run on a GPU.

### Big battle performance: frame profiler, skin cache, shadow geometry
- Measurement first: `PerfStats` (Core) is a frame profiler that also runs in release builds. `-perf [seconds]` or Options.ini `PerfOverlay = yes` prints a `[PERF]` line to stderr every `PerfInterval` seconds (default 5): fps, frame time avg/max, game logic, client update, drawable updates, particle update, draw, render target textures, 3D scene, opaque mesh flush (includes CPU skinning), shadows, particle/sorted flush and present in ms per frame, plus draw calls, triangles, sorted triangles, skinned meshes and vertices, skin cache hits, shadow volume rebuilds (and their triangles), particles and texture memory. `PerfOverlay` also draws three lines in the top left corner. `-perfdump <seconds> [delay]` writes one CSV row per frame of the next match (after `delay` seconds) as `perf_<date>_<time>.csv` into the capture folder. The Windows x64 package gets `Perf.cmd` (`-perf -perfdump 300 60`, stderr to `perf-log.txt`) and an INSTALL.txt section. Only clocks and counters are read; logic, CRCs and replays are untouched.
- What scales with triangles per unit, from the code: (1) W3D skins are deformed on the CPU every time they are drawn (`MeshGeometryClass::get_deformed_vertices`, one bone per vertex) and copied into the shared dynamic vertex buffer (44 bytes per vertex, batches of at most 65535 vertices, a DISCARD lock per batch), whether or not the pose changed; a skin drawn twice in a frame (reflection, shadow texture) is skinned twice. Culled skins are not skinned (`Add_Visible_Skin` only runs from `MeshClass::Render`). (2) Shadow volume geometry: the duplicate vertex search was O(V^2) (118 ms for a 16k triangle mesh) and the polygon neighbor table O(P^2) (1.6 s for 16k triangles, built on the first silhouette in Zero Hour and at spawn in Generals), a stall per new model; caster meshes over 16384 vertices get no shadow volume, and a failed build was repeated for every unit. (3) Silhouette and volume rebuild is O(P) per caster mesh whenever it turns more than 0.2 degrees, so every turning turret or vehicle rebuilds every frame. Skin meshes never cast shadow volumes, so skinned models need a decal shadow in their INI. (4) No distance LOD: `Prepare_LOD` and the predictive LOD optimizer are commented out, so HLods always draw their top LOD; content LODs are ignored.
- Fixes (render only, both games): per instance skin cache (`skinposecache.h`, `MeshClass::Get_Deformed_Vertices_Cached`): the deformed positions and normals are kept with the transforms of the bones the mesh uses; while those are bit-for-bit equal the stored vertices are used and skinning is skipped (output identical). Options.ini `SkinCache = no` turns it off. Shadow geometry: duplicate vertices by sorting, neighbors from a vertex to polygon list with the same candidate order, so both tables are identical (checked against the old code on 62 random meshes with split vertices, flipped and degenerate faces, NaN and signed zero: 1.6 s to 9 ms and 118 ms to 1.7 ms for 16k triangles); failed models are remembered (`Register_Missing`). Caster meshes with at least `ShadowVolumeHighPolyTriangles` (4000) triangles rebuild after `ShadowVolumeHighPolyAngle` (3) degrees instead of 0.2; retail models stay under 4000. `ShadowVolumeMaxTriangles` (default 0 = off) gives heavier models a ground decal shadow instead.
- Regression guard: `scripts/qa/perf/run-skin-cache-bench.sh` builds `skin_cache_bench.cpp` against the cache header only: 60 units with a synthetic 15k triangle skin for 300 frames, checks hits, misses on a moved used bone, hits on a moved unused bone and bit-identical vertices. Here: idle 2.8 to 0.03 ms per frame, a third animated 2.7 to 0.9 ms, all animated unchanged.
- Deferred: per instance GPU vertex buffers written only on a cache miss (the dynamic VB copy still runs every frame), GPU skinning through a d3d8 vertex shader (the skin path uses fixed function FVF; DXVK would handle shaders but the W3D skin renderer and its multi-pass materials would need a new path), distance LOD selection for content HLods, instancing of identical units.
- Checked: the changed files compile with mingw (Zero Hour flags; Generals files with the same flags without RTS_ZEROHOUR). Not yet run on a GPU; the deploy thread measures a big skirmish with `Perf.cmd` before/after.

### FSR 2 reactive mask for particles and smoke
- Problem: particles, smoke, explosions and other alpha-blended effects write no depth and get only camera motion vectors, so FSR 2 kept them in its history and they smeared and left trails.
- Engine (both games): `RTS3DScene::Flush` calls `W3DUpscaler::markOpaqueScene()` after opaque meshes, trees, stencil shadows and water, right before translucent objects, particles and the sorted alpha-blended polygons. While FSR 2 is active and only inside the tactical view's scene (between `beginScene` and `endScene`), it sets private render state 251. Terrain, shroud, decals, tracks, trees and water stay on the opaque side, so they keep full temporal anti-aliasing. Render only.
- DXVK patch `0015-fsr2-reactive-mask.patch`: render state 251 copies render target 0 (resolving MSAA); render state 252 hands that copy to the swap chain only when it came from the same render target (a view filter may draw the opaque scene into a texture). At present, `ffxFsr2ContextGenerateReactiveMask(opaque, scene)` writes an r32f mask (the format the AMD shader writes) that `ffxFsr2ContextDispatch` gets as `reactive`. Flags: tonemap, threshold, max component, as in AMD's FSR 2 sample; the sample's threshold 0.2 is lowered to 0.05 because the game's LDR colour only spans 0..0.5 after FSR 2's tonemap, and 0.2 would only catch the brightest explosions. Marked pixels get `UpscalerReactiveScale` (default 0.9, AMD's recommended maximum reactivity). No transparency & composition mask: the helper does not make one, and FSR 2's experimental auto path would replace the reactive mask.
- Options.ini `UpscalerReactiveScale` (0 to 1, default 0.9, 0 = off) and `UpscalerSharpness` (0 to 1, default 0.5) reach DXVK as `DXVK_FSR2_REACTIVE_SCALE` / `DXVK_FSR2_SHARPNESS`; environment values the player set win. `DXVK_FSR2_REACTIVE_THRESHOLD` tunes the threshold; `DXVK_FSR2_DEBUG=reactive` shows the mask in red over a grey scene (blue tint: no mask that frame), documented in INSTALL.txt.
- Sharpening: with `UpscalerSharpness` above 0, FSR 2 runs its own RCAS pass on the scene, the composite pass runs the FSR 1.0 RCAS (`FSRSharpness`) only on the interface pixels it takes from the EASU image, and the final present pass runs RCAS at 64 stops (lobe 2^-64, no sharpening) so bloom and the colour grade still apply. `UpscalerSharpness = 0` keeps the behaviour of #59.
- Fix in the SPIR-V reflection of patch 0013: glslang keeps resources a pass declares but never reads (`cbFSR2` in the reactive pass, unused textures in others). The backend bound those with empty buffers or images in the wrong layout. Only resources that some instruction refers to are listed now.
- Checked: patches 0001-0015 apply with `git apply` on DXVK v2.6 and build with mingw (d3d8.dll, d3d9.dll exporting `GeneralsX_UpscalerSubmitFrame`); the changed engine files compile with mingw (Zero Hour and Generals). A native DXVK build ran a D3D9 test (opaque checkerboard, moving translucent quad, interface bar) on lavapipe with the Khronos validation layer (AMD's backend needed a test-only change to accept lavapipe's single host-visible memory type): the debug view marks exactly the quad and never the interface; without the mask the quad leaves an orange trail (mean R-B excess 19.6 over the checkerboard), with it none (0.0); interface pixels are identical within 1/255 between the two sharpening paths. The reflection fix removed every per-frame validation error, including three per frame from #59; left are pipeline-creation messages about device-scope atomics in AMD's luminance pyramid on lavapipe. Not yet run on the Windows test PC.

### FSR 2 temporal upscaler (opt-in)
- AMD FidelityFX FSR 2 v2.2.1 (MIT) runs in the DXVK swap chain blitter: new patches `0013-fsr2-temporal-upscaler.patch` (blitter, FSR 2 integration, two compute shaders) and `0014-d3d9-upscaler-inputs.patch` (frame hand-off from the game). The Windows x64 workflow clones FSR 2 at tag v2.2.1 and copies `src/ffx-fsr2-api` into `src/dxvk/fsr2/ffx-fsr2-api` before `meson setup`; without those sources DXVK builds as before and FSR 2 reports unavailable. Linux and macOS do not apply `cmake/dxvk-patches`, so nothing changes there.
- AMD's Vulkan backend and core are compiled as they are. AMD's shader table (`ffx_fsr2_shaders_vk.cpp`, generated by the Windows-only FidelityFX shader compiler) is replaced: meson compiles the 18 GLSL permutations with glslang, and binding names and slots are read back from the SPIR-V at run time. A force-included shim sends the backend's global Vulkan calls through DXVK's instance table and hides fp16 and subgroup size control, which DXVK does not enable on the device.
- Game side (both games): Options.ini `Upscaler = FSR2 | FSR1 | OFF` and `-upscaler fsr2|fsr1|off` (command line wins). FSR 2 is never on by default: without `Upscaler`, RenderScale below 100 still means FSR 1.0. `UpscalerQuality = Quality | Balanced | Performance | UltraPerformance` gives 67/59/50/33 percent when an upscaler is named and RenderScale is not set; RENDER_SCALE_MIN_HEIGHT (600 lines) still applies. `Upscaler = DLSS` logs that DLSS is not available and uses FSR 2. WinMain sets `DXVK_UPSCALER`.
- `W3DUpscaler` (Core) jitters the tactical view's 3D camera with Halton(2,3) only while DXVK reports FSR 2 active (8 x scale^2 phases), through a new `CameraClass` jitter that only `Apply()` uses, so culling, picking and game logic see the unjittered camera. After the 3D scene it passes the unjittered view and projection, the jitter, clip planes, field of view, 3D viewport and frame time to `GeneralsX_UpscalerSubmitFrame` in d3d9.dll and sets render state 252; DXVK copies render target 0 and the depth buffer there, before the control bar, health bars and cursor are drawn.
- At present a compute pass turns depth into camera motion vectors (inverse current view-projection, then the previous one), FSR 2 upscales the scene copy, and a composite pass uses the FSR 1.0 EASU image wherever the final frame differs from the scene copy (the interface), so the interface never enters the temporal history. RCAS still sharpens the result with FSRSharpness; FXAA is skipped while FSR 2 runs.
- Limits: moving units only get the camera's motion (FSR 2's depth clip and locks catch the rest, some smearing on fast units is possible); translucent interface pixels show the jittered scene underneath; FSR 2's descriptor ring assumes at most 4 frames in flight. Diagnostics: `[FSR2]` lines on stderr (choice, render and display size, every 600 scenes) and `FSR2:` lines in d3d9.log.
- Checked: DXVK v2.6 with patches 0001-0014 builds with mingw (d3d8.dll, d3d9.dll exporting `GeneralsX_UpscalerSubmitFrame`); all FSR 2 permutations compile with glslang; the changed engine files compile with mingw. Not yet run on a GPU; FSR 2 becomes the default only after frame comparisons against native 3440x1440 on the Windows test PC.

### Ambient occlusion (SSAO-lite)
- New DXVK patch `0009-d3d9-ssao.patch`. `RTS3DScene::Flush` (both games) sets private render state 253 after the opaque meshes and before trees, shadows, water, translucent objects and particles. DXVK then copies the depth like for soft particles and runs two raw Vulkan passes between the D3D9 draws: 12-sample normal-oriented occlusion from the reconstructed view positions into an R8 image, then a 4x4 depth-aware blur multiplied into render target 0. Units, buildings and props get soft shade where they meet the ground and in creases; the sky, water, effects and the interface are untouched.

### Soft particles
- Smoke, dust, fire and explosion sprites no longer cut a hard line where they cross the ground, a unit or a wall: they fade out over the last 8 world units in front of the geometry behind them.
- New DXVK patch `0008-d3d9-soft-particles.patch`. The game marks its particle draws with private render state 254 (blend mode in bits 0-1, fade distance in bits 8-23). On the first marked draw of a frame DXVK copies the scene depth (sample 0 of a multisampled depth buffer) into a single-sampled image, binds it on sampler 7, and the fixed function pixel shader turns both depths back into view distance with the projection's _33/_43 terms and fades alpha (alpha blend), colour (additive) or colour towards white (multiply). Alpha tested particles keep their edges. On by default; `DXVK_SOFT_PARTICLES=0` turns it off.
- Particles go through `SortingRendererClass`, which draws them later mixed with other translucent triangles, so the state travels with each sorted node and is set right before its draw. A flush holding soft particles stays inside one pass, so the depth is copied once per frame. Core code, so both games get it; without the patched DXVK (Linux, macOS) the state is ignored.
- Checked with native DXVK 2.6 on lavapipe with Vulkan validation on a test scene (ground, a box, a translucent quad sunk into the ground), single-sampled and with 4x MSAA: the quad fades into the ground instead of cutting it, no validation errors.

### Terrain tiles from AI-upscaled HD packs
- How terrain tiles are loaded: each map stores texture classes (name, `numTiles`, `firstTile`). `WorldHeightMap::readTexClass` opens `Art/Terrain/<file>.tga` through TheFileSystem (mod .big files, then loose files, then the retail .big), `countTiles`/`readTiles` cut the sheet into an NxN grid and store every cell as a `TileData` of `TILE_PIXEL_EXTENT` (256) px with a CPU mip chain. `updateTileTexturePositions` packs the classes into one atlas `TEXTURE_WIDTH` (8192) px wide with an 8 px border (`TILE_OFFSET`), so 31 cells per row, 961 cells at most, height rounded up to a power of two. `TerrainTextureClass` is A1R5G5B5 with 3 mips (8192x8192 is 128 MB before mips); blend edges go into an A8R8G8B8 `AlphaEdgeTextureClass` of the same width. Retail sheets have 64 px cells and are already replicated 4x into the 256 px cells since July, so the atlas is the same size for retail and for a 4x pack.
- Problem: grid detection only knew 64 and 256 px cells. A 2x sheet (128 px cells) or an 8x sheet (512 px cells) was read as a grid of 64 or 256 px cells, so only the top-left quarter of the image showed up. A 4x upscale of a 32x32 half tile was read as 2x2 cells as well.
- Fix: the texture class already says how many tiles the sheet holds (N x N, counted from the retail sheet when the map was made). The cell size is now the largest power of two from 64 to 2048 px that still fits N cells across the image: 64 for every retail sheet (same as before), 128 for 2x, 256 for 4x, 512 for 8x, 1024 for 16x. Cells below 256 are replicated up as before (retail path unchanged), 256 px cells are copied 1:1 (full resolution), larger cells are box-filtered down to 256. Trees and WorldBuilder (no class count) keep the previous 64/256 rule; `readTiles` now derives the cell from the grid width when no cell size is passed, which also fixes WorldBuilder reading retail sheets as 256 px cells. On map load stderr gets one `[TerrainHD]` line per sheet that is not 64 px cells, saying the cell size and whether it was upscaled, copied at full resolution or downscaled.
- Not done, on purpose: atlas cells above 256 px. At 512 px cells an 8192 atlas holds only 15x15 = 225 cells (too few for many maps), so it would need 16384x16384 (512 MB at 16 bit before mips, past what most GPUs should spend on one texture), and `TileData` would grow 4x per tile (fixed arrays, roughly 1.4 MB each, a few hundred per map) and would need dynamic storage plus a runtime extent in all UV code (`getUVForNdx`, `getUVForTileIndex`, `getUVForBlend`, `TerrainTextureClass::update`, the alpha edge texture, flat textures). The GPU limit is not changed by this work: the atlas stays at the existing 8192 width whatever the pack holds. Bigger cells would also mostly be lost to the 16-bit A1R5G5B5 atlas format (5 bits per channel), which is the next thing to change for HD terrain (A8R8G8B8 doubles the atlas to 256 MB at 8192x8192).
- For HD packs: same file names under `Art/Terrain/`, uncompressed or RLE TGA, 24 or 32 bit, exactly 2x, 4x or 8x the original width and height (powers of two only, 3x is not detected). 4x is the best size: 256 px per original 64 px cell, shown at full resolution.
- Render-side only: tile pixels feed the terrain atlas and radar colors, not the height map, pathfinding or CRC. The code is in Core, so both games get it. MinGW syntax-only compile for Zero Hour (WorldHeightMap.cpp, W3DTreeBuffer.cpp). Not run with a real pack yet.

### 32-bit terrain tile atlas
- Problem: the terrain tile atlas (`TerrainTextureClass`, up to 8192x8192) and the far-terrain flat textures (`getFlatTexture`, used by `W3DTerrainBackground`) were always A1R5G5B5, 5 bits per colour channel, so the full-resolution 256 px cells from HD packs showed colour banding on gradients. The blend pass (`AlphaTerrainTextureClass`) shares the atlas's D3D texture; the blend edge texture (`AlphaEdgeTextureClass`) was already A8R8G8B8.
- Change (`TerrainTex.cpp`): both `TerrainTextureClass` constructors now create A8R8G8B8 when the device supports it and texture reduction is 0 (texture quality High), and A1R5G5B5 otherwise. `update` and `updateFlat` fill either format through one pixel writer: the 16 bit path is the retail conversion unchanged, the 32 bit path stores the full 8 bits with alpha 0xFF (the retail texels had the alpha bit set, so blending is the same). Tile placement, the 4 px borders, mip generation (`D3DXFilterTexture` box filter, which the CompatLib d3dx8 also does for A8R8G8B8) and UVs are unchanged, so the only difference is precision. `AlphaTerrainTextureClass` creates its throw-away texture in the base texture's format so its recorded format matches the shared texture. Two dead `#if 0` test-pattern blocks in the fill loops were removed.
- Memory decision: an 8192x8192 atlas is 256 MB at 32 bit against 128 MB at 16 bit (plus about a third for the 3 mips); most maps need a 4096 or 2048 high atlas, a half or a quarter of that. Players who chose a lower texture quality (texture reduction 1 or 2) asked to save texture memory, so they keep the retail 16 bit atlas; the format is picked when the map's terrain texture is built, so changing texture quality takes effect on the next map.
- Render-side only, in Core, so both games get it. MinGW syntax-only compile for Zero Hour (TerrainTex.cpp). Not run in game yet. Stacked on the HD terrain tile PR (#30).

### One ~430 ms frame spike in every DXVK video capture
- Every camtest clip had exactly one ~420-435 ms frame and 14 ms frames otherwise, together with the `video writer queue is full` warning. That warning only fires when presentation blocks on a full writer queue, and the clip time stamps are taken at present, so the stall was the capture's own: the writer queue held only 16 frames (~230 ms at 70 fps), and the encoder (ffmpeg starting up and probing on its first frames) or a new file (first writes) stalls once for longer than that at the start of recording. Since the writer keeps up afterwards, it happens once per run.
- `0004-present-video-capture.patch`: the queue now holds 512 MB of frames on 64-bit (128 MB on 32-bit), at least 16 and at most 1024 (about 260 frames, 3.7 s at 70 fps, at the default 50% scale of 1080p), and the writer thread allocates and touches all of it right after opening the output, during the warm-up second before recording, so presentation never allocates. The finish log line now also gives the longest single wait.
- Checked by reading the code: the camtest flow itself was not changed (game logic untouched). The DXVK patch compiles (syntax-only) against DXVK 2.6 with 0001-0003 applied, and 0005 still applies on top. Not run on the test PC yet.

### Fuller toxin, radiation and fire fields at High detail
- Ground-aligned `AREA_EFFECT` particles (the "field" particles that paint toxin, radiation and fire fields) stop spawning once more than `MaxFieldParticleCount` (30) are on screen, so fields looked patchy with a few on screen. `ParticleSystem::createParticle` now uses a cap scaled by static detail level: Low and Medium keep the configured value, High x2, Very High x4. Custom follows the player's particle cap compared with the High and Very High presets. GameLOD.cpp and GlobalData defaults are unchanged.
- Looked at and left alone: texture filtering (particles already use the global filter and anisotropy settings, and forcing trilinear per texture would need render-state overrides that the sorting renderer replays); clamp addressing (particle textures are shared by name with laser and model materials that tile); dynamic LOD particle skipping (it is the frame rate safety valve, and the same dynamic level also drives debris and slow death timing in GameLogic, so it is not touched); soft particles (no depth texture in the DX8 path).
- Particles are client-only (client random stream, no CRC or save data), so gameplay is unaffected. Code is in Core, so Generals and Zero Hour both get it.

### Water texture filtering and a larger sea reflection at High+
- What actually draws water in Zero Hour and Generals: `WATER_TYPE_0_TRANSLUCENT` (rivers and standing water areas via `drawRiverWater`/`drawTrapezoidWater`). Its "reflection" is the base texture plus sparkle and camera-projected noise in a ps.1.1 shader; there is no render-to-texture pass. The planar mirror (`renderMirror` into `m_pReflectionTexture`) only exists for `WATER_TYPE_2_PVSHADER`, which needs bump textures the Zero Hour code no longer loads (`#if 0` in `init`), so it stays off unless a mod sets `WaterType = 2`.
- `setupJbaWaterShader` and `setupFlatWaterShader` wrote MIN/MAG=LINEAR straight to the device. That replaced the anisotropic BEST filter on the base water texture, left the sparkle/noise/edge stages with whatever mip filter was set before (shimmer at a distance), and desynced the `DX8Wrapper` stage cache, so the next stage 0 texture with the same cached filter lost anisotropic filtering too. Stage 0 now keeps its `TextureFilterClass` filter, and the detail stages get trilinear filtering through `Set_DX8_Texture_Stage_State`. In the flat water shader, stage 3 is left to the shroud shader as before.
- Type 2 reflection texture: 256x256 at Low/Medium as in retail. At High/Very High it is the largest power of two that fits the depth buffer's short side, capped at 1024 (1024 at 1440p). If that fails it falls back to 256 with a log line, and the mirror pass and sea draw handle a missing target. It is drawn with `Set_Render_Target_With_Z`, which already goes through `_Set_Render_Target_With_Matching_Depth`, so it gets a single-sampled depth buffer with MSAA on. The size is chosen when device resources are created, so a detail change takes effect after the next device reset.
- The code is in Core, so both games get it. MinGW syntax-only compile for Zero Hour. Not run on a GPU yet.

### MSAA default by GPU class, and MSAA/FSR logging
- Hardware test of the 4x default on a Radeon 780M at FSR 67% (2580x1080 -> 3440x1440): 4x ran at 45 FPS, off at 60 FPS. Without an `AntiAliasing` key in Options.ini, `DX8Wrapper::Set_Render_Device` now lowers the default to 2x on integrated GPUs and keeps 4x on discrete GPUs. An explicit Options.ini value is used as is, and the `CheckDeviceMultiSampleType` fallback still applies to both.
- The GPU class comes from the D3D8 adapter identifier (DXVK fills it from the Vulkan device name and vendor ID): Intel is integrated except Arc A/B cards; AMD is integrated for generic "Radeon Graphics" names, RADV APU chip names (PHOENIX, REMBRANDT, VANGOGH, GFX1103 and so on) and model tokens like 780M or 8060S, unless the name has RX or Pro; NVIDIA is discrete; Apple, software rasterizers and unknown vendors count as integrated. A VRAM heuristic was not used: D3D8 has no dedicated memory query before the device exists, and DXVK reports a large device-local heap on APUs.
- `GlobalData::m_antiAliasLevelIsDefault` (both games) carries whether the key was missing to `WW3D::Set_MSAA_Mode`. Without the key, the Options menu shows the level the device picked, so Accept saves 2x on an integrated GPU instead of 4x.
- stderr gets `[MSAA]` lines: the adapter description, vendor/device ID, class and rule when the device is set up, then one line per device creation or reset with the Options.ini level, the requested level and rule, the actual back buffer MultiSampleType read back from the device, back buffer and window size, and whether FSR is active.
- New DXVK patch `0010-log-msaa-fsr-present.patch` (renumbered from 0006 and rebuilt on top of the 0006 bloom patch) logs the same in d3d9.log: `D3D9SwapChainEx` back buffer size, format and MultiSampleType on every swap chain creation or reset, and a `Swap chain: present` line whenever the FSR state, sizes or back buffer sample count change (FSR active, MSAA resolved before EASU, or FSR off).
- Code is in Core plus the GlobalData/Options menu of both games. MinGW syntax-only compile for Zero Hour and Generals; patch 0006 applies after 0001-0005 on DXVK 2.6 and the changed DXVK files compile. Not run on a GPU yet.

### Reproducible `-skirmish` setup for A/B clips

### Camera-fitted terrain draw size for wide screens
- A Hor+ view plane (retail vertical FOV, wider horizontal FOV) with the TheSuperHackers aspect height boost dropped was tried and reverted: on the deployed 21:9 build the view looked zoomed in, because the boost (x2.06 camera height on 21:9) already shows more ground in both directions than Hor+ at the retail height. ### Sharper projected shadows at High detail
- Projected shadows (`SHADOW_PROJECTION` / `SHADOW_DYNAMIC_PROJECTION`) render the caster's silhouette into one shared render target and copy it into a per-model texture. That target was a fixed 512x512, so the silhouette was blurry and blocky when stretched over the ground. Its size now follows the static detail level: Low 512 (retail), Medium 1024, High, Very High and Custom 2048. If the device refuses a size (format, memory or max texture size), it halves down to 512; the copy into the per-model texture uses the real size.
- The size is picked when the device resources are created and again in the per-map `reset()` (all shadow textures are freed there), so a detail change applies from the next map.
- Checked the rest: stencil (volumetric) shadows are on at High (retail GameLOD.ini) and Very High (`initStaticLODLevels`), use the top HLOD level, and are pixel exact, so they have no resolution to raise. Decal shadows use their art textures with the default (bilinear or better) min/mag filter and no mipmaps, so they always sample the full-size texture; no blur to fix there. GameLOD.cpp and GlobalData were not touched.
- Rendering only, no logic impact. Zero Hour and Generals; MinGW syntax-only compile of both files is clean.
### 4x MSAA by default, working with FSR, pillarbox and screen filters
- The game already had native MSAA from upstream (`AntiAliasing` in Options.ini, `D3DPRESENT_PARAMETERS::MultiSampleType`, `CheckDeviceMultiSampleType` fallback 8 -> 4 -> 2 -> off for the back buffer and depth formats), but it was off unless the key was set. A missing `AntiAliasing` key now means 4x, and the Options menu shows the level through `OptionPreferences::getAntiAliasing()`, so it no longer shows Off and writes 0 on Accept.
- With a multisampled back buffer the FSR patch skipped FSR (EASU samples single-sampled images) and fell back to a plain bilinear blit. New DXVK patch `0005-present-fsr-msaa-resolve.patch` resolves the back buffer with `vkCmdResolveImage2` into a cached image of the same format before EASU, so FSR, PNG capture and video capture (which read the FSR output) all get the anti-aliased image. Without FSR, DXVK's own multisampled present path is used as before.
- DXVK's `d3d9.forceSwapchainMSAA` was not used: it multisamples the swap chain behind the game's back, so the render-to-texture code below would not know the depth buffer is multisampled, and it ignores the in-game setting.
- Render targets that borrowed the multisampled default depth buffer (water reflection, projected shadows, the black and white / motion blur / cross-fade screen filters) now get a single-sampled depth buffer of the same format from `DX8Wrapper::_Set_Render_Target_With_Matching_Depth`, cleared when bound. Before, DXVK silently unbound depth for these passes, and the screen filters were turned off whenever MSAA was on.
- The pillarbox path (game resolution below the back buffer) renders the scene into a multisampled surface and resolves it into the offscreen texture with `CopyRects` (DXVK runs it as `StretchRect`), instead of losing the anti-aliasing in a single-sampled texture. If the resolve fails it logs and continues without MSAA.
- Code is in Core (both games); the Options menu change is in Generals and Zero Hour. MinGW syntax-only compile for Zero Hour; the DXVK patch compiles against DXVK 2.6 with patches 0001-0004 applied. Not run on a GPU yet.

### `-camtest` spawns a convoy and follows it
- The 12 s comparison clips for `SmoothMotion` showed only camera motion, since almost nothing moves at match start. When the flight begins, camtest now spawns 8 ground vehicles of the local player's base side in two columns between the start position and the map center (America: Crusader + Humvee, China: Battlemaster + Gattling, GLA: Scorpion + Technical; a missing template falls back to the other one, then to the American unit) plus 2 helicopters when the side has one (Comanche, Helix). Units face sideways so they turn first, then get `aiMoveToPosition` (CMD_FROM_PLAYER) towards the map center, so they path, turn and accelerate like player orders.
- The camera pivot follows the center of the live ground vehicles instead of the fixed pan: the center is sampled once per logic frame and blended by `FramePacer::getLogicFramePhase()`, then lightly smoothed (0.35 s time constant), so the camera glides at any render rate with `SmoothMotion` on or off. Yaw turn and the final zoom-out are unchanged; with no convoy it falls back to the old fixed pan. `[CAMTEST] convoy:` lines log what spawned and where the convoy ended up.
- Spawning only happens with `-camtest`; it changes the match, so replays recorded with it will not play back. Code is in Core, so both games get it; checked with a MinGW syntax-only compile for Zero Hour and Generals.

### Smooth unit motion between logic frames
- Moving units stepped at 30 Hz at 60 FPS because `Drawable::draw` passed the object's logic transform straight to the draw modules. Each drawable now remembers the object transform of the previous logic frame (captured lazily when it is drawn, keyed on `GameLogic::getFrame()`) and `Drawable::getSmoothMotionTransform` blends it with the current one by `FramePacer::getLogicFramePhase()`: lerp for the position, shortest-arc normalized quaternion lerp for the rotation. Rendering trails logic by up to one logic frame, like the camera, animations and particles that already use the phase.
- No blend (logic transform as is) when the drawable has no object, was not drawn in the previous logic frame (new, hidden, off screen), moved more than 100 world units in one logic frame (teleport), the logic time scale is off, the game is paused, the phase is not inside (0,1), the transform did not change, or `SmoothMotion = no`. Scaled matrices keep the current rotation and only blend the position.
- What follows: the model, its turret, bones, recoil, physics tilt, bone-attached particles, riders and dependency models (they use the render object), shadows and terrain decals (they read the render object transform), the health bar, its icons, the veterancy and formation marks (offset by rendered minus logic position), and the camera when locked on a unit.

Click picking ray-casts the render objects, as retail always did, so it hits the drawn model; it only yields an object ID. GameLogic and the CRC never read the blended transform (the only logic caller of client bone positions is the immobile Particle Uplink Cannon, which never blends).
- `SmoothMotion` in GameData.ini or Options.ini (`yes` by default) turns it off. Implemented in Zero Hour and backported to Generals; `W3DModelDraw` and `W3DView` are in Core.

### DXVK video capture for motion checks
- New DXVK patch `0004-present-video-capture.patch`: `DXVK_CAPTURE_VIDEO` records every presented frame for `DXVK_CAPTURE_VIDEO_SECONDS` (default 10), starting `DXVK_CAPTURE_VIDEO_START` seconds (default 5) after the first present, downscaled on the GPU (linear) to `DXVK_CAPTURE_VIDEO_SCALE` percent (default 50) of the presented size. The test PC's screen is locked, so desktop capture is not possible.
- Frames go through a ring of 4 cached readback buffers and are read once their GPU work is done, then a writer thread with a 16-frame queue writes them; a full queue blocks presentation (logged) instead of dropping frames. The log line at the end gives frame count, average fps and any waits.
- `raw:<path>` writes `<path>.bgra` and `<path>.txt` (`w h`, then one present time stamp in microseconds per frame), for machines without ffmpeg; `scripts/qa/dxvk_video_to_mp4.py` turns them into an mp4 with the real frame timing. Any other value is a command line (`{w}`/`{h}` replaced) that receives Matroska raw BGRA video with the same time stamps on stdin. On Windows it is started with `CreateProcess` (no console window), since `_popen` hangs in GUI programs.

- The DXVK HUD (`DXVK_HUD=fps,frametimes`) is in the video: while recording, the HUD is composited from an image (rendering it twice would add a second point per frame to the frame time graph), and the frame is composed at swap chain size before the downscale so the HUD keeps its place. One second of warm-up frames compiles the extra pipelines before recording starts.
- Checked on lavapipe with a d3d9 test app (alternating 10/30 ms frames): mp4 time stamps match the present times, frame counts match the log, HUD and FSR paths render, a missing encoder stops the capture without affecting the game. The MinGW object compiles.

### 2026-10-17 09:00 UTC: open list cost index for long paths

- The open list counter of e0a01c7 settled logic frame 674: one ChinaTankBattleMaster path of 30392 cells walked 3.7 million open list cells to insert its cells in cost order (about 120 per insertion), 77 ms. The open list now keeps a cost index (`std::map` from total cost to the last cell of that cost on the list), and a new cell goes right after the last cell whose cost is not above its own, which is the place the forward and reverse walks found. A randomized test of 6 million inserts, pops and cost changes against the old walks gives the same list order after every step, so paths, CRCs and replays do not change. Each cell remembers the cost it was inserted with, because the A* code updates the cost just before it takes a cell off the list. The step counter added for this measurement is removed again (Core, both games; `RETAIL_COMPATIBLE_PATHFINDING` builds keep the walks).
### 2026-10-17 08:45 UTC: tank tracks no longer make every frame wait for the GPU

- The new wait counters (e0a01c7) put 18 ms of every frame after the day battle (frame 22 ms, 45 fps) and 25-42 ms during it in one buffer lock: the 480000 byte dynamic vertex buffer of the tank tracks, locked without `D3DLOCK_DISCARD`. The tracks refill the whole buffer every frame, but the plain lock made DXVK wait until the GPU had finished drawing from the previous contents, so CPU and GPU frames never overlapped. The same lock was 52.5 ms of the 143 ms frame at logic frame 674. The lock now discards, like the road, bridge, bib and tree buffers (Core, both games).
### 2026-10-17 08:30 UTC: night hulls no longer wash out white next to flashes

- fx-night15 (5.0 s, 8.0 s) and fx-night17 show tank hulls going flat white or beige next to hits and muzzle flashes. Each effect light lit a model with up to `MAX_OBJECT_LIGHT` (1.0), and the fixed function lighting adds every point light that reaches a model, so a burning tank with three or four flashes on it saturated. `W3DEffectLights::addModelDynamicLights` now adds the dynamic lights of a model and, when their light at the model's nearest point (same 1 / (1 + 6 d^2 / r^2) falloff as the point lights) is above 0.6, scales all of them down for that model only. Rendering only, no logic or CRC impact (Core, both games).

### 2026-10-17 — Terrain black flicker with a still camera

- Artem's bug video (bug1908, nightly 7508c10): the terrain flickered dark in every match while the camera stood still, and cloud shadows moved with the camera.
- Cause: the detail terrain shader (#64) built the cloud, light map and detail texture matrices from the view matrix read back from the device before the pending camera view was sent to it. The device could still hold the previous frame's view or the identity view of a 2D or particle pass. Retail terrain shaders apply pending state first.
- Fix: `getTerrainNoiseMatrices` calls `DX8Wrapper::Apply_Render_State_Changes()` before reading the view. Render only, no logic or CRC impact. Shared Core code, so the base game gets it too.

### 2026-10-18 — Cheaper ambient occlusion in battle

- Battle is GPU bound (the present path dominates; frame-work waits are ~0 and scene work is ~6-10 ms). Of the DXVK post passes, SSAO is the most expensive one with a measured cost (~9 fps on the camera fly-over at 60 vs 51).
- The SSAO occlusion pass now takes 8 samples per pixel instead of 12. The 4x4 interleaved rotation and the 4x4 apply-pass blur stay, so the lower sample count is smoothed the same way; the blur is kept at 4x4 on purpose so it still de-noises the fewer samples.
